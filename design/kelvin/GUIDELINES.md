# Kelvin guidelines

Kelvin is the design system for every app under the 72F name. It belongs to the school of HyperOS, ColorOS and OriginOS (a paper-white page, grey rounded wells, left-aligned titles, full-colour settings tiles, everything rising from the bottom) and adds what is next: shader-driven glass and controls that stretch, heat up and settle like soft matter. Kelvin's glass is its own material, not a copy of anyone's: ground glass with a fine grain, lit from its bottom edge at the accent's colour temperature, with a bevel that bends the edge outward and frost that thaws under a warm finger. How much glass a person sees is their choice, on one stepless slider from near-opaque to fully Clear; the default is the accessible one. Kelvin is iOS-inspired but never iOS-looking, the way HyperOS 4 and ColorOS 17 are. Its signatures are 72F's alone: the **aurora** of colour temperature behind titles, **heat-lit pebbles** that glow at their base in the accent's temperature, **quiet titles with loud numbers**, the **one-handed opening** that holds the title within the thumb's reach, and the **notch** cut into media tiles around their badge. Kelvin's components, shapes and physics are 72F's own; on Android, Liquid glass is rendered by Vitro, an open-source (Apache 2.0) Liquid Glass engine calibrated against iOS 26, and Frost by Kelvin's own shader on Backdrop (Apache 2.0).

## Principles

1. **Paper page, grey wells, glass on top.** Content lives in pale `group` wells on the white `canvas` (near-black wells on black in dark). Glass is only for chrome over content: the dock, the compact title bar, sheets, dialogs, toasts and badges.
2. **One signal colour.** `accent` marks the one thing to act on or the current state. Everything else is `ink`, greys and glass.
3. **Controls are matter.** A pressed glass button squashes and leans toward your finger, a switch knob heats up and pulls like taffy, the dock's droplet follows your finger and stretches with speed. Everything springs back and can be caught mid-flight.
4. **The person sets the glass.** Glass, stretch and wobble scale with one setting, on one stepless slider from near-opaque to fully Clear. Frosted is the default; the system's accessibility settings always win.
5. **Thumb first.** Top-level pages open with a massive centred title that folds into the bar (see One-handed reach). Navigation floats at the bottom in a compact glass dock with the orb budding out beside it; screens without a dock put their controls in a control bar at the foot. Dialogs and sheets rise from the bottom.
6. **Say it plainly.** Every state says what is happening and what to do next.
7. **Interaction heats; rest cools.** Kelvin is the unit of colour temperature, and the system behaves like hot matter (see Thermal). Nothing stays hot, so a still screen is calm and costs nothing.

## Thermal

Kelvin takes its name literally. Heat is its signature, and it follows real physics: matter glows along the black-body curve (`blackbody(kelvin)`), from ember red through orange and yellow to white-hot, and cools back the same way. Every heat colour is anchored on the app's accent (`thermalColor(heat, accent, glassHeat)`: an ember of the accent, then the vivid accent, then `glass-heat`, then white-hot, warm white for warm accents and blue-white for cool ones), so each 72F app glows in its own colour.

- **Touch leaves heat.** Every pressable surface (`kelvinPress`: rows, tabs, tiles, pills) warms where the finger is, over a quarter second, spreading as it is held. On release the spot keeps glowing and cools through the ramp, gone in about a second. Several touches leave several embers. It replaces a flat press sheen; reduced motion falls back to a plain dim.
- **The aurora follows the sun.** The aurora's top-end light is the colour temperature of the hour (`circadianKelvin`): candlelight (1,900 K) through the night, a warm sunrise, blue sky (12,000 K) at midday, a golden evening. It is the light of the room the screen is in.
- **No fire on chrome.** Molten veins were tried on the masthead and on the dock's droplet and dropped after user testing: switching tabs gives a segment tick and nothing else, and the masthead's expression is its weight.
- **Sliders are filaments.** The filled track runs from an ember at its start through the accent to a white-hot tip at the knob, which glows brighter while held. The thick slider runs ember to accent, keeping its label legible.
- **Figures roll.** Changing numbers (`KelvinRollingText`, used by stat cards) roll like an odometer, up as they grow and down as they shrink, on the spatial spring.
- **Haptics.** A segment tick when a selection moves (dock, segmented controls), distinct on and off clicks for switches, a confirm for committed actions (`rememberKelvinHaptics`).
- **Budget.** Heat animates only while something is hot: the embers a touch leaves. Reduced motion turns embers into a plain dim.

## Signatures

- **Layered glass tiles** (`KelvinGlassTile`, used by `KelvinRow`): settings icons are two materials, after HyperOS 3 and ColorOS 16 icons. A vivid shape in the tile colour sits back toward the top-start; over it, offset toward the bottom-end, a pane of frosted glass tinted by what it covers, with a light top edge and the white glyph. Never a flat colour square.
- **Thermal bar chart** (`KelvinBarChart`): every bar is a frosted pill on a shared floor; the chosen one is a heated filament (an ember at its foot through the accent to `glass-heat` at its top, with a glow) with its value above it in `on-accent-soft`. Tapping a bar picks it with a segment tick; heights grow on the spatial spring. Day and category labels sit beneath.
- **Slide to confirm** (`KelvinSlideToConfirm`): a track in the theme's grey (sunken in light, raised in dark) with a knob in the action's colour (accent, or red when destructive) and a white icon. As it slides the two trade colours: the knob blends to white while the track behind it blends to the action's colour, meeting at a midway tone halfway, and ending as a white knob on a track that is all colour. For consequential actions where a dialog feels flimsy (delete, sign out, reset). A white pebble knob carries the action's icon at the start of a grey capsule; dragged, it heats the track behind it (ember through the accent, or `critical` when destructive) and the label fades. Past 85% on release it runs to the end, confirms with a haptic and acts; short of that it springs home carrying the finger's speed; past the end it stretches on the rubber band. TalkBack offers it as one action.

## Not iOS

Apps that copy iOS 26 directly all end up looking alike. Kelvin keeps iOS's structure and changes what people notice first:

- **Material.** Frost and Kelvin's pebbles are the default; Liquid glass, with the iOS 26 refracting rim, is an option a person turns on in Appearance. Never ship Liquid as an app's default.
- **Type.** Titles are calm (`display` 32 at 500), never the bold iOS large title. Expression goes into the centred masthead that folds into the bar and light, large, tabular figures (`hero`, `numeral`).
- **Light.** The aurora brings the app's colour temperature into the page; pebbles carry it at their base.
- **Reach.** A massive centred title opens the page and folds into the bar as it scrolls (One UI); the dock is compact and centred (ColorOS 17); related controls share one capsule (HyperOS 4).

## One-handed reach

- **The masthead.** A top-level page opens with `KelvinReachHeader`: about a third of the screen (at least 240dp), the aurora behind it, the summary centred near its foot. `KelvinReachBar`, laid over the page, draws the title in it: massive (`masthead`, 52/58) in Onest Black (900) and centred at rest, as large as fits. It is the first thing in what scrolls; do not pad by `LocalVeilInset`.
- **The fold.** As the page scrolls up the title shrinks, slims on Onest's variable weight axis from 900 to 600 (in steps of 25), and slides to the top-start corner, landing at `title` size in the bar's 52dp row (eased, following the scroll, never on a timer); the summary fades in the first third, and the veil fades in under the title as it arrives. Actions (up to two circles) stay at the top-end throughout. `KelvinReach.progress` runs 0 to 1; `collapsed` is true once the title has landed.
- **The foot.** Actions live at the bottom: the dock and its orb, or on screens without a dock a `KelvinControlBar` (a leading circle, a centre cluster, a trailing circle) above the navigation bar. Related actions (skip back, play, skip forward) share one `KelvinControlCapsule` instead of standing as loose circles.
- Detail pages keep their back-and-title bar on the veil; the reach opening is for top-level pages.

- **One way in, within reach.** An action lives in one place. Search is the capsule under the opening, never also a circle in the top corner, the hardest spot on the screen to reach one-handed; top-end circles are for actions with no home lower down (an account, a menu).

## Glass

Kelvin glass is modelled, not painted. Everything light does on it comes from geometry and optics in the shader; Kelvin draws no highlights, rims, glints or glows.

- **A real pane.** Each glass element is a pane with a thickness and a rounded rim (flat on top, curving down to the edge as a quarter circle). The view through it is refracted by Snell's law at the curved surface and carried through the thickness, so the rim bends the backdrop inward the way a thick glass edge does, and the flat middle stays true.
- **Dispersion.** Red, green and blue refract with slightly different indices, so strong bends split colour at the rim, as real glass does.
- **Fresnel.** At grazing angles the rim reflects the scene just beyond it (Schlick's approximation). That reflection is the only edge highlight.
- **Frost.** Ground glass diffuses the backdrop into a smooth, saturated blur (colour survives, as on HyperOS and ColorOS) with only a faint fixed grain that stays on the pane while content moves behind it. Toward the opaque end, light glass over dark content stays milky white rather than turning grey. A finger's warmth thaws it (`thaw-radius`), and it fogs back over after release.
- **Carried light.** Light entering through the bottom edge travels up the pane and is scattered out by the frost, tinted `glass-heat` (the accent's colour temperature). It fades as the glass clears, because clear glass does not scatter.
- **The Glass setting** (Settings > Appearance > Glass) is one stepless slider from 0 to 1, labelled Opaque at the left end and Clear at the right; nothing snaps. The left end is near-opaque ground glass (a 94% coat over heavy frost), never a flat pane. Toward Clear the coat thins to nothing, the frost clears, and the pane gets thicker with a wider rim, so refraction grows to its extreme. `glass-frosted` (0.3) is the default; `glass-glass` and `glass-liquid` are reference points on the range, not stops. Coloured controls keep their stain to the end (84% in the middle at Clear), so a CTA still reads as one.
- **Two textures.** Settings > Appearance > Glass first picks the material, Frost (the default) or Liquid glass, then its intensity on its own stepless slider; each texture keeps its own intensity, so switching back restores it. Frost applies everywhere Liquid glass does (the dock, header controls, pills, menus, toasts, sheets, dialogs, badges, glass buttons and the veil).
- **Frost** is deep ground glass, as on the HyperOS lock screen: no refraction, dispersion, reflection or thaw. The page behind melts into one wide blur (44dp at Opaque, 34dp at Sheer), its colour pushed further (saturation 1.3 to 1.8) so a sunset behind glows through as warm light, under a smoky `glass-tint` coat from 96% at Opaque through 62% at the default (0.5) to 34% at Sheer, with a fine fixed grain. It is matte: no rim light and no contour strokes on its panes or pebbles; edges come from the coat and the shadow. Frost never gives up the contrast floor, on sheets and dialogs too, so its slider has no warning. Under Frost the dock's droplet stays a solid drop while dragged, and the veil blurs as deeply as the panes.
- **Clear** is fully transparent: no coating, no frost, the thickest pane and no contrast floor. Show the warning "Clear glass has no contrast protection. Text over photos may be hard to read." beside it.
- **Contrast floor.** Up to Liquid, glass that carries text never lets the backdrop take text below 4.5:1: on light glass the shader lifts anything darker than `glass-floor-light` toward `glass-tint`; on dark glass it pulls anything brighter than `glass-floor-dark` down. Text on glass is `ink`; secondary text is `glass-ink-2`, never `ink-2`.
- **Accessibility always wins.** Android's high-contrast text and the web's "increase contrast" and "reduce transparency" force an opaque pane. Battery saver caps glass at Frosted. Reduced motion stops all deformation.
- **The veil.** Every title bar sits on the veil, top-level or not: the large title with its search and filters, a detail screen's back-and-title bar, the compact bar that fades in on scroll. The page runs the full height of the screen beneath it (`KelvinVeiledScreen`, with the page padding its scrolling top by `LocalVeilInset`). Top chrome that spans the screen is never a pane. It sits on a veil: the page blurs and washes into `canvas` toward the status bar and fades to nothing a `space-8` below the chrome, with no edge. The pane with a refracting rim is for floating chrome only. Text set straight on the veil (the large title) follows what shows through it: `ink` over a light page, white once dark content scrolls under a thin veil, crossing with a short colour fade.
- **Stained glass.** Coloured controls over media (a primary button on a photo) are glass with the colour in its body. Its opacity follows Beer-Lambert absorption along the path through the pane: dense in the thick middle, so `on-accent` keeps its contrast, thin at the rim, where the page refracts through. The stain is the vivid version of the colour (OKLab lightness lifted, chroma pushed to the edge of sRGB), as bright as ColorOS and vivo fills, and the light that passes through it lands on the page as a soft shadow in the same colour. On Android every filled control given a backdrop is Liquid glass: vivid fills (Primary, an Accent icon button) are stained; soft and grey fills (Tinted, Secondary, Danger, Raised, a chosen pill, the orb) are glass in their own tone. Controls with no backdrop stay solid; there is nothing behind them to bend.
- No glass on glass: a sheet over the dock hides the dock.
- **Two settings.** **Glass** is how hard Liquid glass's rim bends the page, from Subtle to Strong (default 0.5); it is the refraction and nothing else. **Transparency** is how much of the page shows through: the coat, blur, veil and contrast floor, on the stepless Opaque to Clear range described above. Under Frost there is only Transparency (Opaque to Sheer).
- Platform renderers: on Android 17 QPR2 (API 37.2) and later, Frost panes and pebbles in the page's window are drawn by the platform's backdrop effects, which see exactly what is under the pane; elsewhere Backdrop draws them from a recording of the page. Either way the result is the same material.
- Platform fallbacks: on Android, Liquid glass is Vitro's, with Vitro's own approximation before Android 13; sheets, dialogs and menus (their own window, which Vitro cannot see out of) get a deep blur under their coat; high-contrast text gets an opaque pane with a hairline edge. The thaw no longer clears Liquid glass on Android. On the web, Chromium refracts the rim through an SVG displacement filter; Safari and Firefox get blur and tint. Thaw is Android-only.

- **What is glass.** Glass is for chrome that floats over content: the dock, header controls on the veil (circle buttons, the search capsule, filter pills, resting ones lit glass and the chosen one accent), menus that grow out of their opener, the selection action bar, controls and captions over photos, the AI composer and its suggestions. Cards, rows, stat tiles and buttons inside groups stay solid; there is nothing behind them to bend.
- **Windows.** Sheets and dialogs that live in their own window cannot see the page, so the system blurs everything behind the window (Android 12+, cross-window blur on) by the Glass level's frost, and the panel is `glass-tint` at the level's coat, never thinner than half.
- **Never glass on its own page.** A page that glass refracts must not contain glass that refracts it; content recorded into a page backdrop sees no page.

## Soft controls

- Every control's deformation scales with the Glass level: light at Frosted, full at Liquid and Clear, none with reduced motion.
- **Pebbles.** Buttons are frosted, matte stones after HyperOS 4's, drawn by Kelvin's own shader, never iOS lenses, and always subtle: a face true to its colour, with all the light in the bevel. Light soaks into the rounded edge and glows softly just inside it, a little more along the top, under a faint broad sheen. Kelvin's trace is a thread of the accent's colour temperature (`glass-heat`) along the bottom rim, barely there in light mode and a little warmer in dark; knobs have none. No rim-light stroke under Frost (Liquid glass, when chosen, keeps its rim light). Beneath a pebble is a whisper of shadow falling straight down (10dp, 3dp, 5.5%); under a vivid fill, a faint haze of its own colour. If a pebble reads as a pebble from across the room, it is too much. Pressed, the button swells by about 4dp, is pulled toward the finger and stretches along the pull, and light blooms where it is touched (less on pale fills, where light would wash them white). Glass buttons also bend the page behind them through a steep, tucked rim. In dark mode the rim light is gentler. Dialogs, sheets, menus and toasts are liquid glass panes, capped at the Glass mark (0.55): past it the slider clears bars and controls further but never the panes that carry reading text. They show the page through a heavy frost and a milky coat of at least 86% (no contrast floor; the pane is large and evenly coated), so the dock or a bright photo behind never shows through as a smear, bending at a thick rim with a touch of dispersion, with the same rim light and a soft shadow. The dock, the search capsule, chips, pills, menus and badges carry the same rim light as glass buttons, so every floating pane reads as one material; only edge-to-edge bars and the dock's clear lens go without. Switch and slider knobs are white pebbles with a soft drop shadow and no glow. Text buttons stay flat.
- **No outlines.** No drawn hairline goes round glass, chips or badges. The only edge light is the liquid-glass rim light, brightest on the two corners facing toward and away from the light, the same on every floating pane; other edges come from refraction, shadow and fill contrast.
- **Press**: buttons swell under the finger (see Pebbles); other elements squash like warm wax (a little wider and lower) while held and spring back with a small wobble. Glass buttons also lean toward the finger as it moves, the pull easing off so they never leave their place, and thaw where they are touched; the backdrop stays aligned behind them.
- **Switch**: held or dragged, the knob lifts, widens toward where it is going, and pulls like taffy past either end before springing home. A flick picks the side it was thrown toward, however short.
- **Slider**: while dragged the knob lifts, swells and stretches with speed. Past either end the whole slider stretches toward the finger and snaps back on release.
- **The bud.** When the orb appears beside the dock it buds out of the dock's end: it starts small inside the end cap and springs out to its place (damping 0.55, a little past and back), a neck of the same glass stretching between them, thinning, and snapping once the gap passes 10dp. At rest there is no neck. Reduced motion places the orb at once; under Liquid glass, which only draws rounded rectangles, it buds without the neck.
- **Dock**: compact and centred, shrink-wrapping its tabs rather than spanning the screen. The selection is a solid `accent` droplet with the icon and label in `on-accent`, Kelvin's mark where iOS greys the current tab; it slides between tabs and stretches with its speed. Dragging along the dock lifts it into a clear glass pane over the icons, refracting them, following the finger on a stiff spring and landing on the tab underneath.
- **Sheets and dialogs** follow the finger down and stretch when pulled up past their height. A flick down dismisses them however short it was.
- **The rubber band.** Anything pulled past its end (a switch knob, a slider, a sheet pulled up) follows one curve, `c·x·d / (d + c·x)` with c = 0.55 (`KelvinMotion.rubberBand`): about half the finger's travel at first, less and less after, never more than `d`.
- **Release carries speed.** When the finger lets go, the spring starts at the finger's speed, never from rest, so nothing stops dead for a frame on release.

## Decoration budget

Each decoration is subtle; together they are a lot. A screen carries **at most two decorative layers at once**: the aurora counts as one, glass as one, embers and pebble finish are feedback and do not count. So a top-level opening has the aurora and its glass chrome and nothing else decorative; a page deep in a list has glass alone. The aurora moves only while the opening is on screen, and the in-app Reduce motion setting (as well as the system's) stills it, which is WCAG 2.2.2's pause control.

## Learnable, never a dead end

Kelvin may be unfamiliar; it may never be a dead end. A pattern people have not seen before (slide to confirm, the folding masthead, an icon-only dock, the glass settings that change the screen as you move them) is fine if a few moments of trying teach it, the way a new dock position becomes the standard within days. What is not fine is a person who, after a little experimenting, still cannot understand something or cannot do something:

- **Every destructive action can be undone** for a few seconds (a toast with Undo), or is confirmed first and says it cannot be.
- **Every mark is explained** where it sits: a line on a chart carries its label, a dot its meaning.
- **What looks tappable is tappable.** Accent words are actions; a figure's accent glyphs (`:`, `/`, `%`) are punctuation, never words.
- **Every action has a visible way in.** Gestures (a long press, a drag) only ever add a shortcut to something also reachable by a plain tap.
- Assistive tech always has a plain route: slide to confirm is one action for TalkBack and Switch Access.

## Which switcher

Four controls look alike; each has one job, and a screen never uses two for the same job.

| Control | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `KelvinDock` | Move between the app's sections | Photos, Albums, Memories, Settings |
| `KelvinTitleTabs` | Switch between whole views of one section, set as its title | Today / Money, Apps / Widgets |
| `KelvinTabs` (filter pills) | Narrow what a list shows; the list stays the same list | All, Images, Notes |
| `KelvinSegmented` | Choose a value of a setting or a scale | System / Light / Dark, Day / Month / Year |

## Settings

Settings is a list of places, not a list of options. Past about a dozen options (count every switch, choice, slider and link), one long page stops being findable: a person scrolls past what they came for, and every new feature makes it worse. So from there Settings becomes a list of categories, each opening a page of its own. Below that, one page under section headers is right.

- **The top page lists categories.** Each is a `KelvinRow` with its glass tile in the topic's colour, a title, a summary and a chevron. Two or three `KelvinGroup`s sort them by kind (how the app looks and reads; what it tells you and keeps; the app itself), with no section headers: the gap between wells does their job. An account or plan card may sit above them.
- **Summaries name what is inside**, not the category: the two to four things a person most likely came for, on one line at the default text size ("Theme, accent colour, glass"), never "Manage appearance settings".
- **A category is a detail page**: circle back button, the `headline` title, then its groups under `KelvinSectionHeader`s. It is pushed with Kelvin's push and leaves by predictive back (`KelvinStack`, or `KelvinTransitions` with `KelvinDestination` in a NavHost). The dock steps aside on it, and Settings keeps its scroll for the return.
- **The same names in every 72F app**, so the same thing is in the same place: Appearance (theme, accent, glass, system font), Accessibility (reduce motion, reading aids), Notifications, Data & privacy (backup, sync, diagnostics, deleting the account), Support, About (version, licences). The app's own categories sit among them: Reading and Music in Rediom, Memories in Gallery. A category of one or two options folds into its nearest neighbour.
- **Every setting lives in one place**, the one a person would open first; never the same switch in two categories. A link to a deeper screen (backup, licences) lives in its category, not on the top page.
- **One level, rarely two.** A category page that passes a dozen options splits into sections; only a tool of its own (backup, a catalog browser) gets a page below a category.

## Colour

- Build screens from `canvas` → `group` → `raised`. In light mode `canvas` is paper white and `group` a pale grey well; raised controls and resting pills on the page are white pebbles on a soft shadow. Text is `ink`; values, summaries and timestamps are `ink-2`; placeholders and disabled glyphs are `ink-3`.
- Light canvas is paper white with pale grey wells; dark canvas is true black with near-black wells.
- Use `accent` for the primary button, the dock's droplet and the orb (the only solid accent fills), switches and radios when on, the dock's droplet behind the current tab, the orb's glyph, links and text buttons, progress, the focus ring and the "72F" half of a wordmark. Put `on-accent` on accent fills. `accent-soft` with `on-accent-soft` is for tinted buttons, the chosen filter pill or tab, the chosen row in a picker, and accent notices.
- **Vibrant, but pleasant, in the dark.** Dark mode keeps every colour's saturation and lowers its brightness: accent, dial, tile and `glass-heat` fills sit about 6% lower in OKLCH lightness than their light-mode twins, status colours 2–3%, the aurora a step softer and `ink` an off-white (#E6E7E9) rather than near-white, so nothing glares on black. Text set in a colour on its soft fill (`on-accent-soft`, the dials' `on-soft`) goes the other way, a little lighter and softer, and holds 4.5:1.
- **Bright in both themes.** Accent and status colours are as vivid as ColorOS and vivo fills: in light mode they sit around 2.2–2.6:1 on the canvas, a deliberate small step under WCAG's 3:1 for UI components, traded for colour; in dark they clear 3:1 comfortably. They are never the only carrier of meaning (status always has an icon or word) and never carry body text. Use them for fills, icons, controls and semibold labels; running text stays `ink` and `ink-2` at 4.5:1 or more. Warm hues are never darkened toward brown to gain contrast.
- **Selection is quiet.** As on HyperOS 4 and ColorOS 17, a selection is marked softly; solid `accent` is kept for the call to action and the dock's current tab. Resting filter pills and tabs are the same material as the buttons around them, with `ink` text at 500:
  - the chosen filter pill or tab is an `accent-soft` wash (glass in that tone over content) with `on-accent-soft` text at 600;
  - over content (header filters, tabs on the veil), resting pills are lit glass: a glass pebble coated in `glass-lit`;
  - on the page, resting pills are `lift` pills (white on a whisper of shadow in light mode, a step lighter than `sunken` in dark);
  - a segmented control (two to four views of the same content) is a grey track whose thumb is a white `lift` stone (a step lighter in dark), the chosen label in `ink` at 600;
  - the one exception is the dock: its current tab sits on a solid `accent` droplet with the icon and label in `on-accent`, which sets Kelvin apart from iOS's grey tab bar; it lifts into a clear lens while dragged;
  - the orb beside the dock is a solid, vivid `accent` drop on its own-colour glow with a white glyph, whatever is behind it, so it invites a touch.
  Glass is never put inside glass to mark a selection.
- **Settings tiles** are full-colour 32px squircles with a white glyph: `tile-blue`, `tile-orange`, `tile-green`, `tile-red`, `tile-violet`, `tile-teal`, `tile-grey`, chosen by topic (each token lists its topics), the same topic on the same colour in every 72F app.
- **Accent dial**: Ember (`accent`), Glacier, Fjord, Fern, Dusk, Rose, Apricot, Ruby, Sage (`dial-*` with `-soft` and `-on-soft`) and Graphite (monochrome). Swap the whole family together.
- **One accent per app.** Every 72F app shares the system and the dial, but each has its own default accent, chosen for what the app is, so the apps are told apart at a glance: HyperMind is Apricot, 72F Gallery is Glacier (the blue of its icon), AppGone is Ruby and Rediom is Sage (a calm reading green); each new app picks the dial colour that fits it. Each app offers the whole dial in Appearance, its own colour first; the wordmark's brand half and the app icon follow the default. A "Match wallpaper" option may take the wallpaper's hue and rebuild the family at the same lightness; neutrals never change.
- Status colours `positive`, `caution`, `critical`, `info` (with `-soft`) only mark status, always with an icon or a word. Destructive actions are `critical`, a true red (#E0141A light, #F2242B dark), never orange `accent` and never coral: a Delete button is a solid red pill with white text, a warning in the person's face; destructive rows set their title in semibold red.
- **The aurora** (the Horizon, `Modifier.horizon`): colour temperature made visible, as soft blobs of light. Six radial glows of different sizes, some stretched into ovals: the accent at the top-start, the hour's light (`rememberCircadianKelvin`) at the top-end, the accent's cool counterpart (blue against Ember, candlelight against Glacier), two neighbours either side of the accent on the hue wheel, and `glass-heat`. In a top-level opening they drift, breathe and turn on unrelated cycles (9 to 19 seconds, so it never visibly loops), overlapping and parting and mixing into new colours where they meet; only while the opening is on screen, never with reduced motion or battery saver. A blob is a gradient, never a shape: it cannot fold, crease or show an edge. The field feathers to nothing toward the bottom. Full strength behind the reach opening and top-level titles, 0.7 behind empty states, 0.5 in sheets. Never under body text in a group.
- `ink`, `ink-2` and `glass-ink-2` meet 4.5:1 on the grounds their notes name, in both themes; accent and status colours trade a little contrast for vividness in light mode (see Bright in both themes). The focus ring is a 2px `accent` outline offset 2px.

## Typography

- **Two faces, one rule: Onest names and counts, Montserrat explains.** Both SIL Open Font License 1.1, bundled and variable on weight. **Onest** (the display face) sets everything that names or counts: the masthead, titles, title tabs, section names, eyebrows, the names inside components (a notice, a toast, an album, a sender), buttons and every figure. **Montserrat** (the text face) sets everything that explains: rows, body, summaries, captions, fields, filter pills and segments, dock labels. Its round, open geometric forms (the Gotham school) give running text a voice SF's grotesque never has. The change of face is itself a level of hierarchy, so a screen reads in two passes: what it is, then what it says.
- **Hierarchy by size, face and colour together**, as OriginOS does, never bold alone. A page's top-level views switch as titles (`KelvinTitleTabs`: the current one in ink, the others the same size at 30%, a short accent bar under the current one). Labels over coloured cards and fields are eyebrows: small, uppercase, tracked wide.
- **The figure grammar** (`KelvinFigure`): numbers large and light; marks (the `/` of a date, the `:` of a time, a range's `–`) and symbol units (%, °, ₹) in the accent, word units (GB, AQI, k) in `ink-2` so no accent word appears that is not an action, units at 40% size on the same baseline (never under 12sp on a small figure). Marks and symbol units use `figureAccent`: the accent itself, darkened only as far as 3.5:1 on the group grey needs, so they stay vivid on large figures (they carry no meaning alone). So `7/2026`, `12:32`, `86%` and `71 AQI` read at a glance and look like nobody else's. Every figure in the system follows it: stat cards, the recorder, chart readouts, slider values, row values with digits, album counts and message times (an accent colon when unread, a quiet one when read). The one figure that is the point of its screen may be set in heat ink (`kelvinHeatInk`, ink warming into the accent toward the baseline). Time with its date is `KelvinTimeLockup`: the time as a hero figure, date and weekday stacked beside it.
- **Quiet titles, loud numbers.** Titles are calm, as in HyperOS 4; figures carry the expression. The masthead is the exception: Onest Black.
- Scale (display face): `masthead` 52/58 in Black (900, slimming to 600 as it folds) · `hero` 64/64 light · `numeral` 40/44 light · `display` 30/36 at 600 · `tab` 28/34 at 700 · `headline` 22/28 at 600 · `title` 18/24 at 600 · `name` 16/22 at 600 · `action` 15/20 at 600 · `figure` 15/20 at 500 · `section` 14/18 at 600 · `eyebrow` 11/14 at 700, uppercase, +0.1em. Text face: `row-title` 16/22 medium · `body` 15/22 · `label` 14/20 semibold · `summary` 13/18 in `ink-2` · `caption` 12/16 · `micro` 11/14 (dock labels).
- **System font.** `KelvinTheme(fonts = KelvinFonts.System)` sets everything in the phone's own sans (the face the person picked in the phone's settings); offer it in Appearance as "System font". The scale, weights, eyebrows and figure grammar stay; only the faces change.
- Figures are proportional, so a 1 never stands apart; a figure that ticks in place (a running timer) uses tabular figures (`KelvinType.ticking`).
- Section names are `section` in `ink-2`, sentence case. Unread items set their name at 700 and show the signal dot after the time. Marks about the message (unread, starred, important, attachment) sit at the end of its lines, unread after the time and the rest at the end of the subject line; marks about the sender (VIP, verified, the account or app it came from) sit on the avatar's corner.
- Sentence case everywhere except eyebrows.
- **Charts lead with their figure.** `KelvinBarChart` puts the chosen bar's value above the chart as a figure (`71 AQI`, with its day beside it); bars are plain and round-topped, a neutral tint at rest and solid accent when chosen, with an optional dashed average. No glow, gradient or frost on data.
- **Coloured cards are categories, not decoration.** A stat card given a tone (`KelvinStatCard(tone = …)`) fills with the tone's soft colour, its label an eyebrow and its value in the tone's ink: income and spending, in and out. Never more than two side by side.

## Layout, shape and depth

- 4dp grid: `space-1` 4 · `space-2` 8 · `space-3` 12 · `space-4` 16 · `space-5` 20 · `space-6` 24 · `space-8` 32. Gutter `space-4`; gap between groups `space-6`; rows at least `space-14`; touch targets at least `space-12`.
- **The dock names its tabs by room** (`KelvinDock(labels = DockLabels.Auto)`): every tab from 420dp wide; only the current tab from 360dp, where icon-only tabs narrow to 46dp and the current one widens to the width of the longest tab name, capped at 104dp (the same width for every tab, so the dock keeps one width as the droplet moves; a name longer than the cap ends in an ellipsis, so a dock of two long-named tabs never grows absurdly wide), the widths flowing with the droplet as it moves, the icon gliding up and the name fading in only as the droplet settles (never a grey half-name, never a reflow); icons alone below 360dp or with text above 130%. The names stay for TalkBack throughout. `Always`, `Active` and `Never` override.
- **Top-level screen**: the masthead (aurora, a massive centred title, actions at the top-end) → search capsule → tabs → groups → the compact, centred glass dock (64dp per tab, shrink-wrapped) with the accent selection droplet, and the orb budding out beside it for the screen's creation action. The title folds into the top bar as the page scrolls. **Detail screen**: circle back button, then the `headline` title, on the veil; on scroll the compact bar fades in on the veil with the title centred.
- Continuous (G2) corners on every rounded surface, Kelvin's own curve with 60% smoothing: `radius-xs` 8 (icon tiles), `radius-sm` 12, `radius-md` 16 (fields, notices), `radius-lg` 24 (cards), 28 (group wells), `radius-tile` 22, `radius-xl` 32 (sheets, dialogs), `radius-full` (buttons, chips, the dock, the orb).
- **The notch**: a media tile may carry one round glass badge in its bottom-end corner, with the cover cut away around it by a `space-1` moat.
- **Groups are wells with barely-there dividers.** A group is one continuous `group` well with 28dp continuous corners (HyperOS 4); between its items it draws a divider as ColorOS does, but barely there: one physical pixel of `ink` at 6% (8% in dark), inset to where the item's text starts (60dp after an icon tile, 80dp after a message's avatar, 16dp otherwise; `Modifier.groupDividerInset`) and stopping 16dp short of the end. Only the first and last rows meet the well's corners, so a press stays inside them. `KelvinGroup` does this for whatever it holds.
- **Cards side by side share one height.** Stat cards and other cards in a row take the tallest card's height (`KelvinStatRow`), labels at the top and details pinned to the bottom, so both edges line up. Ragged heights belong only to photo and video timelines, where a little chaos adds to the experience; in an app's own cards they read as a mistake.
- **Carousels** (a sideways row of chips, pills or swatches) never cut their contents off. They run to the edge of their card or the screen with their padding inside the scroll and are never faded at their edges: a chip cut off cleanly by the edge is what tells a person the row scrolls sideways. They leave room above and below for a chosen pill's glow (`Modifier.glowRoom`).
- **No Material, Miuix or other kits on screen.** Every control, sheet, dialog, chip, slider, switch, progress indicator, divider and snackbar is the Kelvin one (`KelvinSlider`, `KelvinSwitch`, `KelvinSpinner`, `KelvinDivider`, `KelvinToast`...). Apps may keep Material's theme plumbing underneath for libraries that read it, never its components.
- **Tips** (`KelvinTip`): a one-time pointer to something on the screen, shown once and dismissed for good. A card inset like the dock and always clear of it, the topic's icon on an `accent-soft` disc, a semibold title, a sentence or two in `ink-2`, and a tinted "Got it" at the end. Glass over content, else `raised` on a soft shadow. One tip per screen at a time; never a full-width slab, never under the dock.
- Groups never cast shadows. Raised circles use `shadow-raise`; the dock, orb and toasts `shadow-float`; sheets and dialogs `shadow-sheet`.

## Motion


- **Direction follows the hand.** A back swipe dismisses the screen the way the finger moves: from the left edge it leaves to the right, from the right edge to the left, and the screen beneath comes in from the edge the swipe began at. A push, and a pop by a back button, use the reading direction: in from the end, back out toward it. Pushing is popping played backwards.
- **Predictive back, premium.** As a back swipe starts, the screen lifts into a card (continuous 32dp corners, a growing shadow, a slight squash wider than tall, a lean toward the finger's height) and travels with the finger, frosting as it goes; the screen beneath grows in from the edge the swipe began at, a dim lifting off it, over the canvas (never black). The screen beneath stays composed while covered (undrawn, inert, hidden from TalkBack), so nothing is rebuilt and its glass never flashes when it returns. On release the card springs on and a tick confirms; a cancelled swipe wobbles home. `KelvinStack` has all of it built in; NavHost apps use `KelvinTransitions` (push, pop, directional tab pans, predictive pop, finger-tracked and linear) and wrap each destination in `KelvinDestination` for the card, squash and frost. Reduced motion snaps. Apps opt in with `android:enableOnBackInvokedCallback="true"`.
- **Tabs are places side by side.** The bar that picks them (the dock, title tabs, a segmented control, filter pills) is a map of them in order, so switching pans the view toward the tab picked: pick one to the right and the old content slides off to the left as the new one comes in from the right; pick one to the left and it all runs the other way. Both travel on one spring with no bounce (damping 0.9, stiffness 360), the old fading early so the two never read as one. Dock tabs are whole screens: they pan a quarter of the width and frost as they leave, clearing as they land, as screens do in the stack. A pane within a page pans an eighth, without frost, and the page eases to its new height. Right-to-left mirrors the bar and the pan; a second pick mid-pan turns it from where it is; each tab keeps its scroll and fields while another shows. Reduced motion swaps in place. `KelvinTabContent(selected, travel = TabTravel.Screen | Pane)` has all of it; NavHost apps pass `KelvinTransitions.tabEnter(dir)` and `tabExit(dir)`; on the web, a dock with `data-k-panels` pans a `data-k="panels"` container.
- **Chrome stays put.** The dock, the orb and the toasts never move with a tab switch: they are the hand's place, and the page moves under them.

- **Follow the finger, never flip.** Whatever moves under a finger carries its state with it: words take the accent's ink as the filter pill slides over them, segment labels weight toward the thumb, title tabs brighten as their bar arrives, a slider's icon and value change ink as its fill actually crosses them, slide to confirm trades colours between knob and track along the way, a sheet's scrim lifts as the sheet is pulled down. Nothing changes state at a threshold the person cannot see.
- **Everything pressable moves like a pebble.** Buttons, a control capsule as one piece (pressed or dragged anywhere on it, its play and skip only wash), plain icon buttons, Text buttons, action bar items, filter pills and swatches swell, are pulled toward the finger and stretch along a drag, then wobble back (`Modifier.kelvinPull`).
- **What slides can be dragged.** A segmented control takes a drag as well as a tap: the thumb follows the finger, ticks as it crosses each option and settles on the nearest.
- **Figures roll, ticks swap.** A digit rolls when it changes; one that changes faster than a roll (a timer's hundredths) swaps in place, and every digit rolls inside its own line.
| Spring | Damping | Stiffness | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| snappy | 0.82 | 700 | Press, lift, switch knob |
| spatial | 0.76 | 420 | Things that move: the droplet, sheets, pages, tile to viewer |
| gentle | 0.90 | 260 | Things that change: size, colour, the compact bar |
| wobble | 0.55 | 400 | A liquid control settling after release |

- Animate with springs; the only timed animation is a 120ms fade for content swapping in place.
- Scroll containers overscroll with an elastic stretch. Opening a tile grows it into the viewer from where it sits. Predictive back scales the page to 0.92 and rounds it to `radius-xl`.
- Reduced motion replaces every spring with a snap and turns off deformation.

## Iconography

- **Tabler Icons** outline (MIT), stroke 1.5 on the 24 grid: 24 in the dock and circles, 20 white in settings tiles, 22 in rows, 18 in buttons. Icons take the colour of the text beside them; no emoji.

- **Filled by default.** Kelvin draws Tabler's filled glyphs wherever one exists (`KelvinTheme(icons = IconStyle.Filled)`); line glyphs (arrows, chevrons, plus, minus, x) stay lines, as in every filled set. Outline is the alternative setting. Solid glyphs sit with the glass tiles' coloured shapes.
- **Light, not loud.** A filled glyph in plain ink would be the heaviest thing on the screen, so neutral icons are drawn as light as they can go while holding 3:1 on the group grey. Coloured icons keep their colour; icons on a fill are white.
- **State is never colour alone.** A toggled icon changes form too: an empty star is an outline, a starred one filled. A message in a status colour is led by a glyph whose shape says what it is (`KelvinNote`): a warning triangle for an error or a caution, a tick in a circle for success, an "i" for information, so it reads for people who do not see red from green. Field errors, code errors and the glass warning use it.
- The **signal dot** is an 8px `accent` circle for unread, new or updated.

## Voice and copy

- Write from the person's side: "Your photos are backed up", not "Sync job completed". Buttons are verbs: "Back up now", "Delete 3 photos". Dialogs pair "Cancel" with the exact action.
- Every state names what is happening and the next step. Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, without apology.
- Sentence case, no exclamation marks, no emoji. Numbers carry units: "2.4 GB", "18:42".

## Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)

- **Coloured text is legible text.** Fills stay vivid; text set in a colour goes through `legibleOn(ground)`, which keeps the hue and chroma and moves only OKLab lightness until it meets 4.5:1. Status text in pills, notices and tone cards, Text buttons, danger rows, field errors, avatar initials and `on-accent-soft` (figure marks and units, the chosen pill) all do.
- **Controls are named by their row.** A `KelvinRow` hands its title to the control it holds (`LocalControlLabel`), so TalkBack reads "Reduce motion, switch, off". Controls outside a row take a `contentDescription`.
- **State is visible.** The chosen bar, the droplet, selected pills and an on switch are solid accent. An off switch is a pale track with a white knob, below 3:1 on the group: a deliberate exception (a ring was tried and read as out of place); its position and the row's words carry the state.
- **Text can double.** Controls that hold a line of text grow with it (`Dp.withTextScale`): chips, pills, fields, search, segmented controls, slide to confirm and the reach opening. With text above 130% (`largeText()`) the dock goes icon-only and chart days shorten to their initial. Avatar initials are sized to the avatar.
- **Targets** are at least 48dp in the dock, rows, switches and buttons; chart bars are at least 42dp wide (WCAG 2.5.8 asks 24).
- **White on accent clears 3:1.** Content on an accent fill (the orb's icon, the dock's current tab, primary buttons) is white, and every accent and dial fill is tuned deep enough for white to clear 3:1 (Ember #FD5F12 in light). Small white text on a fill stays under 4.5:1: a deliberate brand choice, the same one ColorOS and OriginOS make.

## Plans and payments

Every 72F app sells and thanks the same way (`Plans.kt`). Kelvin draws; the app keeps billing.

- **Offered, never pushed.** A plan opens from a tap on something it adds, or from Settings, and its hero names that thing first ("Picture search is part of Plus."). Upsell cards (`KelvinPlanCta`) sit where they fit, can be hidden, and never show to someone who has the plan. No popups.
- **The free app is complete.** Say what the plan adds, never what the free app lacks; say what stays free.
- **One thing to tap.** The purchase bar's button, with the price in it ("Get Plus · ₹699"). Restore is always beside it. Loading holds the button; an unavailable store says why and what to do.
- **Plans are radio cards** (`KelvinPlanPicker`): the radio drawn in the card, the price a figure at the end ("₹99/mo", ₹ and / in the accent), one plan badged ("Best value"), an introductory offer in the accent with when it ends. The plan the person has is marked current and cannot be chosen.
- **Prices come from the store.** A preview price stands in until the store answers, and the page says it is a preview.
- **Owned is a state, not a sale** (`KelvinPlanOwned`): the rosette, the plan's name, thanks, and Manage for a subscription.
- **Tips unlock nothing, and say so** (`KelvinTipJar`). They come after everything the plan does: a row each with an accent price button, or a grid of a few short tips. A tip with no price yet dims.
- **Marks** (`KelvinPlanMark`) sit beside what a plan adds: the sparkle and the plan's name, or the sparkle alone inside a chip. Gone once it is owned.
- Each app wears its own accent here as everywhere: no plan colour of its own.

## Building with Kelvin

- **Android**: the `studio72f.kelvin` Compose library, on AndroidX Compose foundation and UI plus Backdrop (Kyant) as the glass renderer, all Apache 2.0. Kelvin's look comes from its own shaders and components running on Backdrop's pipeline; no Material and no third-party UI kit. Wrap the app in `KelvinTheme(dark, accent, glass, reduceMotion)`; read tokens from `Kelvin.colors`, `Kelvin.space`, `Kelvin.shape`, `Kelvin.type`, `Kelvin.motion`. Put `Modifier.kelvinBackdrop(backdrop)` on the page and pass the same backdrop to the glass components over it (`KelvinDock`, `KelvinCompactBar`, `KelvinSheet`, `KelvinDialog`, `KelvinToast`, `KelvinTip`).
- **Web**: load `tokens.css`, `bundle.css` and `bundle.js`, add `class="k-glass"` to glass surfaces and `data-k="press|switch|slider|dock|glass"` to interactive ones, then call `Kelvin.mount()`. `Kelvin.setTexture('liquid' | 'frost')` picks the material and `Kelvin.setGlass(level)` sets its intensity; each texture's intensity is saved separately. For a settings page, mark the picker `data-k="texture"` with `data-texture` buttons and the slider `data-k="glass"`.
- The Glass level lives in each app's Appearance settings and Reduce motion in its Accessibility settings; both persist per device.
